# Halden Group Expenses and Purchasing Policy

Version draft-1

## 1. About this policy

**§1.1 Purpose.** This policy sets out how Halden Group pays for business spend, whether an
employee asks to be reimbursed through an expense claim or a supplier asks to
be paid through an invoice. It explains what may be claimed, what evidence is
needed and who may approve the spend.

The finance team checks every submission against this policy. Where a
submission does not meet it, the finance team records which part was not
met, and an approver decides whether to pay.

**§1.2 Who this policy covers.** The policy applies to every employee of every Halden Group company, and to contractors who have agreed to work under it. Anyone who approves a submission is responsible for checking it against this policy before deciding it.

**§1.3 Grades and figures.** Every employee holds one of the grades associate, manager or executive. Where a limit depends on grade, the claimant's grade on the date of the spend applies. All figures in this policy are in GBP and include any sales tax.

## 2. Principles for spending

**§2.1 Business purpose.** Halden Group pays only for spend that was incurred solely for its business
and that the employee could not reasonably have avoided. Every submission
states its business purpose in enough detail for an approver who was not
there to see why the spend was needed: what the activity was, and who it
was for or with.

A purpose such as "business", "meeting" or "sundries" on its own is not
enough, and nor is a purpose that does not fit the spend, such as a
training course given as the reason for a hotel in a different city on a
different date.

**§2.2 Value for money.** Spend Halden Group's money as carefully as your own. Choose the cheapest option that meets the business need, and do not upgrade travel, rooms or meals for personal comfort.

**§2.3 When to claim.** Submit expense claims within 60 days of the spend. Late claims may still be paid, but the claimant explains the delay and the approver decides.

**§2.4 Weekend and public holiday spend.** Spend on a Saturday, a Sunday or a Halden Group public holiday is unusual
and needs a written justification in the submission explaining why the work
could not take place on a working day. A justification that only restates
the purpose does not meet this requirement.

Acceptable reasons include a client event fixed for that day, an incident
that needed someone on site, or travel that could only be booked for that
day. Convenience, catching up on work or a personal preference are not.

## 3. Receipts and evidence

**§3.1 Receipts.** Every item of spend over £20 needs an itemised receipt
showing the supplier, the date, what was bought and the amount paid. A card
statement or payment slip alone is not a receipt. Electronic receipts and
clear photographs of paper receipts are accepted.

If a receipt has been lost, the claimant says so in the claim and describes
the spend. The approver decides whether to pay it.

**§3.2 Receipts must match the claim.** The amount, date and supplier on the receipt must agree with the claim, and
the items on the receipt must be the items claimed for. A receipt for a
different amount, date, supplier or purpose does not support the claim.

Where a receipt also covers personal items, the claimant marks the business
items and claims only those.

**§3.3 Claiming once.** Each item of spend is claimed or invoiced once only. An employee must not claim spend already paid by a company card, by a supplier invoice or by another claim, and a supplier must not invoice twice for the same goods or services.

## 4. Category limits

**§4.1 Limits by category and grade.** A claim must not exceed the limit for its category and the claimant's grade.
Limits are ceilings, not allowances: claim only what was actually spent.

| Category | Limit applies per | Associate | Manager | Executive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meals and subsistence | day | £35 | £45 | £60 |
| Hotel accommodation | night | £140 | £160 | £200 |
| Client entertainment | person attending | £50 | £70 | £90 |
| Taxis | journey | £40 | £40 | £60 |
| Local public transport | journey | £75 | £75 | £75 |
| Stationery and small office supplies | item | £50 | £50 | £50 |

## 5. Meals and subsistence

**§5.1 When meals can be claimed.** Meals can be claimed when business takes an employee away from their usual workplace for more than 5 hours. Meals taken at or near the usual workplace, including during internal meetings, are a personal cost.

**§5.2 Meals with colleagues.** When colleagues travel together, each claims their own meals. Where one person pays for the group, the claim lists everyone who ate, and the limit applies to each person.

**§5.3 Alcohol.** Halden Group does not pay for alcohol. This covers drinks with a meal, in a hotel bar, on a train or at a team event. Any alcoholic drink on a receipt is removed from the amount claimed.

**§5.4 Tips and service charges.** A tip or service charge of up to 10% of the bill may be claimed on a meal. When the tip was added at the time of payment, the amount paid will be higher than the itemised receipt total; the difference is not a mismatch, provided the payment slip shows it.

## 6. Travel and accommodation

**§6.1 Hotels.** Book a hotel only when the business day makes it unreasonable to travel home. The nightly limit covers the room and breakfast; an evening meal is claimed as subsistence. Minibar, films and other extras charged to the room are personal costs.

**§6.2 Taxis and local transport.** Use public transport where it is practical. A taxi may be claimed when public transport is not available, when carrying heavy equipment, or when travelling late at night, and the claim says which applies.

**§6.3 Trips that span a weekend.** When an approved business trip runs over a weekend or public holiday, the trip itself justifies the employee's meals, hotel and local travel on those days. Quoting the trip approval in the submission is enough; no further explanation is needed.

## 7. Client entertainment

**§7.1 Who counts as a guest.** Client entertainment means hospitality given to people from outside Halden Group for a clear business reason. The claim names the guests' organisation, says how many people from each side attended and explains the business reason. Hospitality for Halden staff alone is not client entertainment.

**§7.2 How the limit applies.** The client entertainment limit is a limit per person attending, counting Halden staff as well as guests, at the grade of the employee who claims. The total claimed is divided by the number of people attending.

**§7.3 Alcohol at client events.** Alcohol served as part of a client entertainment meal may be claimed when the claimant's line manager approved the event in writing before it took place. The claim quotes that approval, and the whole bill, including the alcohol, stays within the per-person limit.

## 8. Foreign currency

**§8.1 Accepted currencies.** Claims and invoices can be submitted in GBP, EUR or USD. Spend in any other currency is claimed in GBP with the card statement showing the converted amount.

**§8.2 Company exchange rates.** Foreign currency amounts are converted to GBP at the company rate for the date of the spend, or the invoice date for an invoice. The finance team publishes these rates and they are the only rates used for limits, thresholds and payment.

**§8.3 Stated exchange rates.** Where a submission states the exchange rate used, and that rate differs from the company rate for the same currency and date by more than 3%, the submission is queried.

**§8.4 Receipts in a foreign currency.** A receipt in a foreign currency will not show the GBP amount claimed. It still matches the claim if the amount in its own currency agrees with the original amount on the claim.

## 9. Purchasing and suppliers

**§9.1 Approved suppliers.** Goods and services are bought from suppliers on the Halden Group approved
supplier list, and invoices from any other supplier are not paid without
review.

A team that needs a new supplier asks the procurement team to assess and add
it before ordering. A supplier is not approved until the procurement team
has added it to the list, even while its assessment is under way.

**§9.2 Purchase orders.** Any purchase over £1,000 needs a purchase order raised and approved before the order is placed. The employee who needs the goods or services raises the purchase order against their cost centre, and the supplier's invoice quotes the purchase order number. A purchase order raised after the invoice arrives does not meet this requirement.

**§9.3 Splitting purchases.** A single requirement must not be divided into smaller orders or invoices to stay under the purchase order threshold or an approval limit. Invoices from one supplier to one cost centre within 30 days whose combined value exceeds the purchase order threshold are treated as one purchase.

## 10. Budgets and approval

**§10.1 Budgets.** Spend is charged to a cost centre, and must not take that cost centre's spend for the year over its budget. The budget year starts on 1 April. The cost centre owner is responsible for finding the money, through a budget transfer agreed with the finance team, before committing to spend that would.

**§10.2 Who can approve.** A manager may approve submissions up to £2,500 and an executive up to £25,000. Spend above that is approved by the Group board outside this process. Associates do not approve submissions.

**§10.3 Approving your own spend.** Nobody may approve a submission for spend they are accountable for: their own expense claim, or an invoice against a purchase order they raised or a cost centre they own.

**§10.4 Payment without manual approval.** A submission of £100 or less that passes every check in this policy may be paid without an approver. Anything larger, or anything that raises a question under this policy, goes to an approver.

**§10.5 Standing approvals.** Some spend recurs on the same terms, such as a monthly travel pass for a role that visits sites every week. A manager may give a standing approval for it, and each later claim then quotes the standing approval instead of restating the business purpose.

**§10.6 Standing reasons recorded by approvers.** An approver decides each submission on its own facts. When an approver accepts a submission because of a lasting fact about a supplier or an employee, such as a supplier that is part way through being approved, or an employee whose role routinely involves weekend work or recurring site visits, that reason may be shown to approvers of later submissions from the same supplier or employee. It informs their decision and never makes it.

## 11. Booking travel

**§11.1 How to book.** Rail, flights and hotels are booked through the Halden travel desk, at least 14 days ahead where the trip is known in advance. Bookings made by the travel desk are paid centrally and are never claimed. Changes and cancellations also go through the travel desk.

**§11.2 Class of travel.** Rail travel is standard class. Flights are economy, except that an executive may approve business class for a flight scheduled to last more than 6 hours.

## 12. Gifts and hospitality received

**§12.1 Declaring gifts.** Any gift or hospitality offered to an employee by a supplier, client or other outside party with an estimated value over £50 is recorded in the gifts register promptly, whether it was accepted or not.

**§12.2 Gifts that must be refused.** Cash, vouchers and any gift offered while a contract with the giver is being tendered or negotiated are always refused. Small promotional items, such as branded stationery, and food shared with a whole team need not be declared.

## 13. IT equipment

**§13.1 Requesting equipment.** Laptops, phones, monitors and other IT equipment are requested from the IT service desk, which buys, configures and records them. Equipment bought personally is not reimbursed.

**§13.2 Returning equipment.** IT equipment remains Halden Group property and is returned to the IT service desk when it is replaced or when the employee leaves. Lost or stolen equipment is reported to the IT service desk on the same day.

## 14. Conference and course fees

**§14.1 Booking conferences and courses.** Conference, course and exam fees are booked by the people team and paid centrally. Fees over £500 need an executive's approval before booking.

**§14.2 Cancelling.** An employee who cannot attend a booked conference or course tells the people team as early as possible, so that the place can be transferred or the fee recovered.
