# Precedents annotate findings and never let a submission skip human review

Long-term memory stores precedents, which are approver dismissals with a reason. A precedent can only be written from an authenticated approver's disposition, and only for codes whose dismissal can rest on a standing fact about the employee or supplier: `NON_APPROVED_SUPPLIER`, `PURPOSE_UNCLEAR` and `WEEKEND_SPEND_UNJUSTIFIED`. A precedent only ever annotates a finding or informs the recommendation. It never removes, downgrades or reroutes a rule finding. When a judgement check is cleared because of a precedent, the clearance is recorded in the audit trail, shown to the approver, and sends the submission to human review. Precedents are matched by subject and code (never by semantic similarity across people), and they stop applying when the text of their clause changes.

We gave up the larger win, memory reducing review volume, because suppressing findings would let one lax or mistaken approval turn into a standing policy exception that nobody sees, and would let anyone who can influence approvals poison memory. Standing exceptions belong in the company records as deliberate changes, such as adding a supplier to the approved list, not inferred from history.
